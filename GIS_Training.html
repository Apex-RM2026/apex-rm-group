A local urban planning office wants a basic GIS asset inventory to understand how physical assets are distributed in one central Kigali neighbourhood. You are the GIS technicians. In two hours you will identify, digitise, organise, analyse and map a small sample of urban assets.

How to set a manageable study-area boundary

  1. Anchor on the two landmarks. The boundary must contain St. Étienne Cathedral and the Belgian Peacekeepers Memorial.
  2. Follow features you can see. Draw the boundary along clearly visible road edges, kerbs or plot edges around the landmarks, so anyone can recognise where it runs.
  3. Match the screenshot's extent. Keep roughly the same area as the screenshot. Do not extend into areas you cannot see clearly.
  4. Keep it small. The area should contain enough features to meet the targets above, and no more. A smaller area means you finish on time.
  5. Document it. Name it AOI-01 Study area (AOI = area of interest). In your map's notes, describe it as "a classroom study area defined from visible landmarks", never as an official boundary.

Two imagery sources: why they may not match

Google Earth Pro imagery

  • Used for digitising (Modules 1–2).
  • Compiled by Google from several providers; the acquisition date is shown in the status bar ("Imagery Date") and older dates are available through View › Historical Imagery.
  • Subject to Google's own terms of use.

Esri World Imagery basemap

  • Used as the background in ArcGIS Pro (Modules 3–7).
  • A compilation of satellite and aerial imagery from different providers; source, date and resolution vary from place to place.
  • Subject to Esri's terms of use and must be credited on the map.

The two sources may have different acquisition dates, resolutions, viewing angles and licences. A building visible in Google Earth may be missing, newer or shifted slightly in Esri World Imagery. A small offset after import is normal and is not an error in your work. Never "correct" your features by guessing; record any mismatch in a Notes field.

Introduction and study-area identification

10 min · 0:00–0:10Google Earth Pro
Module goal: understand what you are going to build and why, and find the study area in Google Earth Pro.
Expected output: Google Earth Pro open and zoomed to the study area, with the four asset types recognised on the imagery.

What is GIS digitisation?

Digitisation means tracing real-world features from a source (here, satellite imagery) to create vector data: geometric shapes with coordinates that a GIS can store, measure and analyse.

A spatial asset is a physical object that has a location and value to an organisation, such as a building, a road segment, a park or a public facility.

Why digitise assets?

  • An image is only pixels; a digitised asset is a record you can count, measure and query.
  • Each record can carry attributes (facts in a table), such as an ID, type and condition.
  • Once assets are in a database, planners can map them, measure them and test questions like "Which buildings are within 100 m of a road?"

Points, lines and polygons

Every vector feature uses one of three geometry types. Choose the geometry that matches how the asset will be used at this map scale.

Digitisation in Google Earth Pro

25 min · 0:10–0:35EssentialGoogle Earth Pro

Before you start: the rules of good digitising

Do

  • Digitise at a consistent zoom (roughly the same eye altitude for all buildings).
  • Trace buildings along the visible roof edge, placing a vertex at each corner.
  • Draw roads along the centre of the carriageway, one segment between two junctions.
  • Place facility points on the centre of the identified building or site.
  • Stay inside the study-area boundary.
  • Use the naming convention below for every feature.

Don't

  • Draw features you cannot clearly see (hidden by trees, shadow or blur).
  • Guess building use, condition or road names.
  • Overlap polygons, or draw one polygon around several separate buildings.
  • Click-and-drag while drawing (this creates hundreds of unwanted vertices).
  • Work in a tilted 3D view.

Why? The database will be used for counting and measuring. Every feature you invent or exaggerate becomes a false "fact".

Naming convention (meaningful and machine-friendly)

Each name starts with a 6-character code: a 3-letter prefix, a hyphen and a 2-digit number. You may add a short label after a space. In Module 4, ArcGIS Pro will read the first 6 characters to create the Asset_ID automatically, so the format must be exact.

Worked example: one building

Why a file geodatabase?

A file geodatabase (.gdb) is a folder that ArcGIS manages as one database. It stores feature classes (collections of features with the same geometry type and the same attribute fields) and tables, with defined coordinate systems and field types. It keeps the asset inventory organised, consistent and ready for analysis.

Why convert KML?

KML is designed for display and exchange: one file can mix points, lines and polygons, and its attribute structure is limited. ArcGIS Pro can draw a KML layer, but to run geoprocessing tools on it, the KML must first be converted to features inside a file geodatabase. That is what KML To Layer does.

Coordinate reference systems: why WGS 1984 UTM Zone 36S?

Geographic vs projected

A geographic coordinate system (GCS), such as WGS84, stores positions as latitude and longitude in degrees. Degrees are not a constant ground distance, so they are unsuitable for measuring metres and square metres.

A projected coordinate system (PCS) flattens the earth onto a grid measured in metres. WGS 1984 UTM Zone 36S (EPSG:32736) uses the same WGS84 datum, measures in metres and is designed for the zone 30°E–36°E in the southern hemisphere.

Is it right for Kigali?

Kigali lies just east of 30°E and about 2° south of the equator, which places the city centre in UTM zone 36, southern hemisphere. Because the 30°E zone edge is close, verify it: in Google Earth Pro, hover over your study area and read the longitude in the status bar. If it is greater than 30°E, zone 36S is correct for your extent.

The data is WGS84 and the target is also WGS84-based, so no datum (geographic) transformation is needed.

Why the projected coordinate system matters

Area is a measurement in square metres. In WGS84 the coordinates are in degrees, and the ground size of one degree changes with latitude, so planar area in degrees has no useful meaning. Projecting to UTM Zone 36S gives coordinates in metres, so the area is in m².

For the instructor: how much distortion is there in Kigali?

Every projection distorts somewhere. Close to the equator, both Web Mercator and UTM distort distances in Kigali by only a fraction of one percent, and UTM's scale factor here is roughly 1.0009 because the city lies about 3° west of the zone's central meridian (33°E). For classroom building footprints this is negligible. We still project because (1) Calculate Geometry Attributes does not support planar area in geographic or Web Mercator coordinate systems, (2) UTM 36S is a documented, standard metric system for this region, and (3) the habit protects students when they work at higher latitudes, where Web Mercator area distortion becomes very large.

Automating asset mapping with ModelBuilder

20 min · 1:25–1:45Key learning outcome
Module goal: build Urban_Asset_Mapping_Model, which repeats the core analysis in one click.

Running tools one by one (Module 5)

  • You open each tool, fill the parameters and run it.
  • Good for exploring and learning.
  • To repeat it with new data, you must remember and redo every step in the right order.

Automating with ModelBuilder

  • ModelBuilder is a visual programming tool: you draw the workflow as a diagram in which the output of one tool becomes the input of the next.
  • The model is saved, documents the workflow and runs again with one click.
  • No code is written.

The model you will build

ConnectionFromTo tool parameterWhy this connection
1BuildingsCalculate Geometry Attributes › Input FeaturesArea only makes sense for polygons.
2RoadsBuffer › Input FeaturesThe proximity zone is built around lines.
3Buildings (2) – output of Calculate GeometrySpatial Join › Target FeaturesUsing the updated output forces the area calculation to run first, so Area_m2 is filled in the result.

Why Spatial Join and not Select Layer By Location inside the model? Select Layer By Location produces only a temporary selection; you would need an extra Export Features step to save it. Spatial Join writes a saved feature class in one step and keeps all buildings with a Join_Count field: 1 = intersects the buffer, 0 = does not. It is simpler for beginners and easy to map.

Why is Project optional? Our inputs are already in WGS 1984 UTM Zone 36S (Module 3). Projecting again would only duplicate data. Add a Project step only when a model's input could arrive in WGS84 or Web Mercator; the simpler alternative is to project once, before modelling, as we did.

What makes a map professional and readable?

Purpose first

The title says what, where and (in the note) when. Every element helps the reader answer the planning question.

Visual hierarchy

Data stands out; the basemap stays in the background. Use transparency and limit colours to one per asset type.

Honest and credited

State that it is a digitised sample, give imagery dates and credits, and avoid claims the data cannot support.

Clean layout

Aligned elements, consistent fonts, white space around the map, no overlapping text.

Readable legend

Plain-language layer names, no internal field or file names, no unnecessary entries.

Measurable

Metric scale bar, north arrow and coordinate system so the map can be used and checked.

Instructor notes

Preparation · pacing · troubleshooting

Before the class (day before)

  • Google Earth Pro and ArcGIS Pro installed and licensed on every machine; ArcGIS Pro 3.x recommended (Export Features is used in Module 4).
  • Every student account can sign in to ArcGIS Pro and display the Imagery basemap.
  • Folder C:\Kigali_GIS exists and is writable on every machine.
  • Reference screenshot copied to each machine (students load it in the study-area panel).
  • Backup KMZ: you have digitised the same AOI yourself, following the naming convention, and saved it as Kigali_Urban_Assets_BACKUP.kmz.
  • Backup project: a completed Kigali_Assets.gdb built from the backup KMZ (for students who fall behind in Modules 4–6).
  • Full run-through completed once on a lab machine in under 100 minutes.

Pacing and catch-up strategy

  • 0:35 – anyone without a KMZ uses the backup KMZ and continues with Module 3.
  • 0:50 – demonstrate Step 3.7 (map coordinate system) on the projector instead of waiting.
  • 1:05 – students behind on fields: complete Buildings and Facilities only; demonstrate Roads/Green_Spaces.
  • 1:15 – demonstrate Analysis A; students do B and C.
  • 1:25 – students behind on data: open the backup geodatabase so everyone builds the model.
  • 1:40 – the model must be running; skip the optional parameter step.
  • 1:57 – everyone exports the PDF, even if polishing is incomplete.

Common problems and fixes

SymptomLikely causeFix
Basemap gallery empty / grey mapNot signed in or offlineSign in to the ArcGIS account (top right of ArcGIS Pro); check the network.
KML To Layer output is missing a geometry typeNo features of that type, or part of the work was exported from a sub-folderRe-export the parent folder from Google Earth Pro and run the tool again.
Features appear in the ocean or far from KigaliDefine Projection was used instead of ProjectDelete the output; run KML To Layer again and use Project.
Calculate Geometry offers no area or gives an errorLayer is still WGS84/Web Mercator, or the field is TextUse the layers from Kigali_Assets.gdb (UTM 36S); make Area_m2 a Double.
Buildings and Green_Spaces contain the same featuresExport Features expression not changedRe-run Export Features with the correct expression (overwrite).
Calculate Field filled only some rowsA selection was activeMap › Clear, then re-run.
Model tool stays greyMissing parameter or broken connectionDouble-click the tool, look for the red error icon, fix, then Validate.
Model fails on second runOverwrite option off, or output open in another programStep 6.1; close attribute tables of outputs; Validate; Run.
Big offset between features and World ImageryDifferent imagery sources/dates, or wrong location digitisedCompare with the screenshot; if the location is right, record the offset in Notes.
